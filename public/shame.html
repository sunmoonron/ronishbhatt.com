Without Blame or Praise

Pornography is femicide, obviously.

  1. It is built on economic coercion, where poverty, inequality, and lack of options funnel women and girls into sexual exploitation marketed as "choice."
  2. It eroticizes male dominance and female submission, turning inequality and abuse into entertainment and sexual education for millions of users.
  3. It industrializes the depiction of women's pain, humiliation, and degradation as pleasurable, eroding empathy and normalizing cruelty.
  4. It trains consumers to see women's bodies as consumable products rather than as expressions of personhood, dignity, and autonomy.
  5. It facilitates and masks trafficking, abuse, and rape, because there is no reliable way for viewers to distinguish coerced content from "consensual" content.
  6. It shapes sexual scripts for men and boys that prioritize domination, conquest, and disregard for boundaries or mutual respect.
  7. It pushes ever more extreme content to capture attention, escalating depictions of violence, racism, and misogyny as routine sexual stimulation.
  8. It reduces women's value to their capacity to be sexually consumed, undermining their safety, credibility, and status in every other sphere of life.
  9. It undermines healthy intimacy by replacing reciprocal relationships with solitary, commodified release detached from responsibility or care.
  10. It launders misogyny through "fantasy," allowing people to deny real-world impact while their habits reinforce hostile attitudes and behaviors.
  11. It saturates digital culture, making it nearly impossible for young people to grow up without internalizing porn-shaped expectations of sex and gender.
  12. It collectively erodes the conditions for women to live free from male violence, because a culture that sexualizes abuse will never fully protect its targets.

Feminists don't believe above, simply because of feminicide.

  1. It monetizes sexual access to women's bodies, turning vulnerability into a revenue stream rather than a reason for protection.
  2. It teaches that women's boundaries are flexible, negotiable, or irrelevant if a man's arousal is at stake.
  3. It collapses the distinction between pleasure and harm, presenting violence as just another sexual preference.
  4. It rewards producers who push ethical limits, because the most shocking, degrading content drives the most traffic.
  5. It grooms viewers to disconnect a performer's visible distress from their own moral responsibility as consumers.
  6. It conditions men to expect sexual access on demand, undermining the practice of mutual consent in real relationships.
  7. It reinforces racist and colonial fantasies that eroticize domination over women from marginalized groups.
  8. It hides the long-term physical and psychological damage to performers behind brief, edited scenes of "enjoyment."
  9. It normalizes environments where sexual harassment, assault, and manipulation are treated as occupational hazards.
  10. It buries stories of regret, coercion, and abuse under marketing narratives of empowerment and choice.
  11. It blurs the line between simulated and real abuse so thoroughly that viewers become indifferent to which they are watching.
  12. It participates in a continuum of male violence that begins with entitlement to women's images and extends to entitlement to their lives.

Men especially are ignorant, I too am vice-filled.

  1. Many people encounter pornography before they have the maturity or education to critically evaluate what they see.
  2. Digital platforms are engineered to maximize engagement, making it easy to stumble into harmful content and hard to step away.
  3. Schools, families, and communities often fail to provide comprehensive, ethical, and relational sex education.
  4. Loneliness, isolation, and lack of connection can make parasocial, screen-based intimacy feel like the only option.
  5. Social norms trivialize or joke about porn use, signaling that critical reflection is unnecessary or prudish.
  6. Advertising and entertainment industries normalize hypersexualized imagery, blurring the line between mainstream media and porn.
  7. Many users have histories of trauma or neglect and seek relief in patterns that were never freely chosen.
  8. Economic and work pressures leave people exhausted, making numbing or escapist habits more appealing.
  9. Powerful corporate interests profit from addiction-like usage patterns and invest heavily in keeping users hooked.
  10. Algorithms personalize and reinforce viewing habits, gradually shifting norms without conscious awareness.
  11. Open, shame-free spaces to talk about sex, desire, and confusion are rare, so people turn to search engines instead of relationships.
  12. Most people were never shown a compelling, lived example of mutual, tender, and just sexuality to measure anything else against.

Sex workers think differently, their work is.. survival.

  1. It can be a response to immediate needs like housing, food, healthcare, or supporting children when other options are blocked.
  2. It may offer higher short-term income than low-wage jobs that still fail to cover basic living costs.
  3. It can be one of the few forms of work accessible to people excluded from formal employment by discrimination or criminal records.
  4. It sometimes provides flexible hours that accommodate caregiving responsibilities or chronic health conditions.
  5. It can be a strategy to escape abusive homes, partners, or environments when no safe shelters or resources exist.
  6. It may be entered under pressure from partners, family, or third parties who control money, documents, or safety.
  7. It can be chosen as the least harmful option in a context of limited, dangerous, or exploitative alternatives.
  8. It is often shaped by global inequalities that push people from poorer regions into markets controlled by wealthier ones.
  9. It is sustained by legal and social stigma that make it harder to leave, report abuse, or access public services safely.
  10. It can involve complicated mixtures of agency and coercion that don't fit simple narratives of "choice" or "victimhood."
  11. It is navigated with strategies for harm reduction, solidarity, and survival built within and between communities of workers.
  12. It reveals the failure of societies to guarantee economic security, bodily autonomy, and safety without commodifying sex.

I am working now. I am broken, how?

  1. I have used pornography to avoid confronting my own loneliness and emotional pain.
  2. I have treated real people as images in my head instead of as full human beings with needs and boundaries.
  3. I have pretended my choices were harmless because I did not want to face their impact on others.
  4. I have hidden my habits from people I respect, knowing they did not match the values I claim to hold.
  5. I have let shame keep me silent instead of taking responsibility and seeking help.
  6. I have confused momentary relief with genuine comfort, even when it left me feeling emptier afterward.
  7. I have ignored the stories of those harmed by the industry because they made my consumption uncomfortable.
  8. I have underestimated how repetition shapes my desires, expectations, and view of relationships.
  9. I have delayed meaningful work and growth by numbing myself with screens and fantasy.
  10. I have used "stress" and "boredom" as excuses instead of learning healthier ways to cope.
  11. I have benefited from systems of inequality while telling myself I was just an isolated individual.
  12. I am capable of changing my habits, telling the truth, and living in a way that better honors others and myself.

Porn remains on Earth, we are charged to suffer.

The Myths We Must Unlearn

  1. "Everyone is always ready and willing" — erases the reality of consent, hesitation, and changing minds.
  2. "No one needs aftercare" — erases the need for emotional check-ins, comfort, and repair after intense experiences.
  3. "Pain equals pleasure" — erases the difference between negotiated kink and non-consensual harm.
  4. "Bodies never have limits" — erases exhaustion, injury, chronic illness, and the right to stop.
  5. "Talking kills the mood" — erases how communication builds trust, safety, and real intimacy.
  6. "Jealousy and insecurity are weaknesses" — erases the vulnerability and reassurance relationships actually require.
  7. "Only appearance matters" — erases character, kindness, reliability, and mutual care.
  8. "More extreme is more exciting" — erases the value of gentleness, patience, and mutual pacing.
  9. "Real life should look like a scene" — erases awkwardness, laughter, negotiation, and slow learning.
  10. "People exist to satisfy my desires" — erases the other person's inner world, history, and boundaries.
  11. "Nothing sticks with anyone afterward" — erases trauma, regret, dissociation, and long-term impact.
  12. "Desire excuses everything" — erases responsibility, accountability, and the moral weight of our choices.
