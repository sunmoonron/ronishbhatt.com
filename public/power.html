How the instrument reads you

The arithmetic of standing.

This is an interpretive model, not a survey. It encodes a specific argument about how power works: that it is multiplicative, not additive, and that a handful of mechanisms compounding together separate the top hundred from the rest by a chasm no single fortune can bridge.

The method

The instrument works in log-power. Your net worth supplies a base — its order of magnitude, since money itself is distributed across orders of magnitude rather than evenly. Onto that base, each mechanism adds "decades" of power: a force that multiplies what your money can do rather than merely adding to it.

Because the mechanisms multiply, holding many of them at once is worth far more than the sum of its parts. A final synergy term rewards exactly that stacking — the reason real apex power looks like one person who simultaneously owns the capital, the network, the information, the megaphone, and the rulebook.

The resulting index is mapped onto the global population through a curve calibrated to real landmarks: the global median, the one-percent threshold, the bracket of roughly sixty thousand who out-hold half of humanity, the ~3,400 billionaires, and the single trillion-dollar fortune at the summit. The mapping is deliberately steep at the top — climbing from rank 100,000 to rank 100 demands a far greater leap of power than climbing from 100 million to 100,000, even though both are thousand-fold jumps in headcount.

What this is and isn't. A reflective illustration, not a measurement. Net worth is self-reported and unverifiable; the mechanism weights express a point of view; per-country figures are rough approximations drawn from public wealth reports. Treat the number as a mirror for thinking about how power concentrates, not as a fact about you.

Privacy. Every calculation runs locally in your browser. Nothing you enter is transmitted, stored, or seen by anyone.
